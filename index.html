Today, product is marketing and marketing is product.

I've spent ten years in SaaS, on both the marketing and product sides. The biggest thing I've learned? The distinction between product and marketing is tiny, if it exists at all.

The best companies make the two work together. Product drives marketing, marketing informs the product. A cohesive spirit. That's my philosophy on how to succeed.

Judgement, taste and just giving a sh*t.

Today, anyone can build anything. Your codebase isn't special. But differentiation is actually easier than ever before.

Every website looks the same. Every post, every article sounds like it was written by Jean-Claude van Chatbot.

You don't need a degree from Stanford or a genius mind to stand out. All you need is good judgement, good taste, and a natural inclination towards caring about what you put out into the world.

If you care how your product looks, care about UX, care about appearances and quality, you're already ahead of 80% of the market.

Everything is UX.

I've spent most of my tech career working in tiny startups, where there are no strictly defined roles. I've been the SEO guy, the content guy, the marketing lead, the product manager.

But if I had to define myself, it would be as a UX expert. Because my core belief is that everything is UX. Whether you're building a website, writing an article, running an ad campaign or coding an app, the only thing that matters is the end user's experience.

It's also the part that AI can't understand. And that makes it scarce. We're about to have a generation of products that look great and work poorly. There are millions to be made by companies that actually care about their user.

That's my focus. I might be writing content, building product or shaping the overall strategy, but at the end of the day it's all just UX.
