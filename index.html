Today, product is marketing and marketing is product.

I've spent ten years in SaaS, on both the marketing and product sides. The biggest thing I've learned? The distinction between product and marketing is tiny, if it exists at all.

The best companies make the two work together. Product drives marketing, marketing informs the product. A cohesive spirit. That's my philosophy on how to succeed.

The edge is judgement, taste, and simply caring.

When everyone has the same tools, the moat isn't the tools. It's judgement — knowing what to build and what to cut. It's taste — knowing when something is actually good. And it's caring, which is rarer than it should be.

If you care how your product looks, care about UX, care about appearances and quality — you're already ahead of 80% of the market.

It all comes down to UX.

Product, marketing, advertising — I approach all of it UX-first, because in the end it's all the same job: making the experience clear, considered, and worth someone's attention.

With what AI now lets you build, the bottleneck isn't shipping — it's the eye. The taste to make what you ship feel intentional. That eye for UX is the thing that sets the work apart.

How I actually build.

Most of what I build runs on clean HTML/CSS/JS or Astro, with a headless CMS — usually something like Pages CMS — handling the content.

A lot of building well with AI comes down to actually understanding the code you're shipping. I've got a solid grip on HTML and CSS, a working level of JavaScript, and enough React, PHP, and SQL — plus the underlying development principles — to steer it and know when something's off.

I build with Claude or Codex, giving clear, specific direction so what comes out has a clean, distinctive style on top of a sustainable codebase — not generic AI output.
